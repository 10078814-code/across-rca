01 / Overview

Nature in Residence : Overview

Overview

Humans are biophilic. We have an instinctual desire to connect with other living beings. But our lifestyles, shaped by contemporary urban planning and architecture, deprioritises this connection. Nature in Residence aims to increase our awareness of the impact that we have on the planet and reinvigorate our relationship with nature. By reinterpreting the idea of an artist residency, the project positions nature as long-term habitat within the RCA’s Battersea Campus architecture.

05 / Guide

How to fill a gap

A step-by-step guide of how to build your own bio-habere.

  1. 01

    Identify the opening

    Identify a suitable architectural gap and confirm its function with the site manager.

  2. 02

    Measure and observe

    Record width, height and depth, then log sun and rain. Use the measured Battersea opening as a starting point.

  3. 03

    Consider target species

    Match plants to soil depth and exposure. Check which insects could use the food or shelter provided.

  4. 04

    Test the bio-habere

    Test how the bio-habere fits within the selected gap. Check its stability, positioning and drainage.

  5. 05

    Confirm installation conditions

    Ask the site manager to confirm the proposed location, any restrictions and the permitted support method.

  6. 06

    Project Longevity

    To sustain the microhabitat, monitor your bio-habere with semi frequent checks.
